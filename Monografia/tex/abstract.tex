The increasing frequency of extreme weather events in central Brazil exposes the fragility of overhead power distribution networks, demanding a shift from reactive maintenance paradigms toward predictive approaches based on artificial intelligence. This work develops and evaluates predictive models to estimate the daily number of power supply interruptions in the Federal District of Brazil, integrating meteorological data from the National Institute of Meteorology (INMET), outage records from the National Electric Energy Agency (ANEEL), and energy consumption data over 3,073 consecutive days (2017--2025). A temporal feature engineering pipeline with 41 derived features was built, including lags up to 7 days, exponential moving averages, and cyclic harmonic encodings. Three predictive architectures were compared under strict temporal separation protocol (Out-of-Sample): eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory networks (Bi-LSTM), and Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Units (Bi-GRU), implemented in PyTorch. Results show that XGBoost achieved the best overall performance ($R^2 = 0.520$, MAE $= 52.3$, MAPE $= 16.1\%$), favored by explicit feature engineering and robust regularization against extreme events. Recurrent networks, although inferior in aggregate metrics, exhibited more concentrated residual distributions, indicating higher accuracy on normal operation days. A multi-horizon analysis (1, 3, 7, and 14 days ahead) revealed a hierarchy inversion: Bi-LSTM outperforms XGBoost for single-day prediction (MAE of 29 vs. 71), while XGBoost regains leadership from three days onward, an effect attributed to error propagation in the autoregressive recursive strategy of neural networks. Multivariate correlation analysis confirmed that precipitation ($r = 0.54$ monthly), wind direction ($r = 0.59$ monthly), and energy consumption ($r = 0.48$ monthly) are significant predictors of interruptions, with effects amplified at broader temporal scales. The study contributes a novel integrated dataset and a replicable methodological framework for distribution network failure prediction, guiding the transition toward predictive maintenance in Brazilian utilities.
