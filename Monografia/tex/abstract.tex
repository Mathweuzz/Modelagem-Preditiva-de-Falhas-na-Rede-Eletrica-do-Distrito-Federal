The increasing frequency of extreme weather events in central Brazil exposes the fragility of overhead power distribution networks, demanding a shift from reactive maintenance paradigms toward predictive approaches based on artificial intelligence. This work develops and evaluates predictive models to estimate the daily number of power supply interruptions in the Federal District of Brazil, integrating meteorological data from the National Institute of Meteorology (INMET), outage records from the National Electric Energy Agency (ANEEL), and energy consumption data over 3,073 consecutive days (2017--2025). A temporal feature engineering pipeline with 40 derived features was built, including lags up to 7 days, exponential moving averages, and cyclic harmonic encodings. Three predictive architectures were compared under strict temporal separation protocol (Out-of-Sample): eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory networks (Bi-LSTM), and Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Units (Bi-GRU), implemented in PyTorch. Results show that, under a unified direct $h=1$ protocol --- where all models receive the same historical features at day $t$ to predict day $t+1$ --- XGBoost (MAE $= 61.1$, $R^2 = 0.409$, MAPE $= 20.6\%$) and Bi-LSTM (MAE $= 62.7$, $R^2 = 0.420$, MAPE $= 20.9\%$) achieve descriptively close performance (2.7\% difference in MAE), while Bi-GRU ranks third ($R^2 = 0.156$). A causal multi-horizon direct forecasting analysis (1, 3, 7, and 14 days ahead, 352 identical target dates per horizon) revealed that recurrent networks outperform XGBoost at medium and long horizons: Bi-LSTM leads at $h=3$ (MAE$=74.0$), while Bi-GRU leads at $h=7$ (MAE$=73.6$) and $h=14$ (MAE$=64.3$). XGBoost achieved $R^2 = -0.027$ at $h=7$, indicating performance below the mean baseline. Multivariate correlation analysis revealed that precipitation ($r = 0.54$ monthly), wind direction ($r = 0.59$ monthly), and energy consumption ($r = 0.48$ monthly) exhibited the highest correlations with interruptions, with effects amplified at broader temporal scales. The study contributes a novel integrated dataset and a replicable methodological framework for distribution network failure prediction, guiding the transition toward predictive maintenance in Brazilian utilities.
