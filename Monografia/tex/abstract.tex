Forecasting the number of power interruptions can support the planning of maintenance crews and resources. This work compares XGBoost, a Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory network (Bi-LSTM), and a Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit network (Bi-GRU) to estimate the daily number of interruptions starting in Brazil's Federal District. The experiments use 3,073 consecutive days, from January 2017 to May 2025, combining records from the Brazilian Electricity Regulatory Agency (ANEEL) with weather data from the Brazilian National Institute of Meteorology (INMET). After data preparation, 3,066 observations and 40 weather, historical, and calendar variables were used. Training and test data were separated chronologically, and the models were evaluated on the same dates. The comparison also included persistence, a reference that repeats the latest observed value. For next-day forecasting, XGBoost achieved a mean absolute error (MAE) of 61.47 interruptions per day, compared with 68.59 for persistence. Bi-LSTM and Bi-GRU produced similar results, and the uncertainty analysis did not indicate a conclusive difference among the three models. An exploratory XGBoost analysis produced a lower error when using only interruption history and calendar variables. In this case, adding the available weather variables did not improve the forecast. Because this combination was chosen after observing the test results, the finding requires confirmation with new data. Forecasts for 1, 3, 7, and 14 days ahead were also evaluated. XGBoost had the lowest MAE for one day ahead, and Bi-GRU had the lowest values at the other horizons. All models produced lower observed MAEs than persistence. The results show that no model was best in every setting and that an association between weather and interruptions does not imply better forecasts or a causal relationship. This work presents an auditable dataset and a reproducible process for preparing data, training models, and comparing results over time.
