Weather and environmental events can affect overhead electricity distribution networks, motivating predictive approaches to support operational planning. This work develops and evaluates models for estimating the total daily number of interruptions in Brazil's Federal District by integrating INMET observations and ANEEL interruption records over 3,073 days (2017--2025). The target was not filtered by cause and contains 748,542 deduplicated events. The pipeline produces 40 input variables, 33 of which are derived, with lags at 1, 2, 3, and 7 days, exponential moving averages, cyclic calendar encodings, and sine/cosine components of wind direction. XGBoost, Bi-LSTM, and Bi-GRU were compared under strict temporal separation. At $h=1$, Bi-GRU achieved the lowest MAE (60.36) and MAPE (18.89\%), whereas Bi-LSTM achieved the lowest RMSE (98.70) and highest $R^2$ (0.421). In direct multi-horizon evaluation, Bi-GRU had the lowest MAE at $h=1$, $h=7$, and $h=14$, while Bi-LSTM had the lowest value at $h=3$. At the monthly scale, wind-direction components ($r=-0.522$ and $0.570$), precipitation ($r=0.539$), and energy consumption ($r=0.476$) had the largest associations, without causal interpretation. Reconstruction from raw files, an integrity manifest, tests, and artifact generation were consolidated into a reproducible single-command pipeline.
