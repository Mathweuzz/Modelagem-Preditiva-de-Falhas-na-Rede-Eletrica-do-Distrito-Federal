The increasing frequency of extreme weather events in central Brazil exposes the fragility of overhead power distribution networks, motivating predictive approaches based on artificial intelligence. This work develops and evaluates models to estimate the daily number of power supply interruptions in Brazil's Federal District, integrating INMET meteorological data, ANEEL outage records, and energy consumption data over 3,073 consecutive days (2017--2025). A temporal feature engineering pipeline with 40 derived features was built, including lags up to 7 days, exponential moving averages, and cyclic encodings. XGBoost, Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM), and Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (Bi-GRU) models were compared under strict out-of-sample temporal separation. At $h=1$, with the same variables per time step and the same temporal cutoff but tabular input for XGBoost and sequential input for the recurrent networks, XGBoost (MAE $=61.1$, $R^2=0.409$, MAPE $=20.6\%$) and Bi-LSTM (MAE $=62.7$, $R^2=0.420$, MAPE $=20.9\%$) produced close results and lower MAE than persistence (68.6). In direct multi-horizon evaluation (1, 3, 7, and 14 days; 352 common target dates), Bi-LSTM had the lowest observed MAE among trained models at $h=3$ (74.0), and Bi-GRU at $h=7$ (73.6) and $h=14$ (64.3); all trained models had lower MAE than persistence at these three horizons. At the monthly scale, precipitation had $r=0.54$, the sine/cosine components of circular wind direction had $r=-0.52/0.57$, and energy consumption had $r=0.48$; for the main climate signals, magnitudes were larger at aggregated scales, without causal interpretation. The study contributes an integrated dataset and a replicable framework for distribution-network interruption forecasting.
