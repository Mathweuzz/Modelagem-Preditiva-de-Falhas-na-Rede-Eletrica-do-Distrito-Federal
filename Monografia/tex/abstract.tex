This study develops and evaluates models to estimate the daily count of unique interruption identifiers whose start dates were recorded in Brazil's Federal District. The dataset covers 3,073 consecutive days, from January 2017 to May 2025, combining meteorological data from the Brazilian National Institute of Meteorology (INMET) and records from the Brazilian Electricity Regulatory Agency (ANEEL); monthly SAMP energy consumption is used only for exploratory analysis. Feature engineering produced 3,066 valid observations and 40 weather, historical, and calendar predictors. XGBoost, Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM), and Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (Bi-GRU) models were compared under strict chronological separation, common target dates, and persistence as a reference. In the main 365-day test for $h=1$, their MAEs were 61.47, 61.74, and 62.10, respectively, compared with 68.59 for persistence. Paired contrasts from 10,000 circular block-bootstrap replicates with seven-day blocks did not distinguish the three trained models; intervals against persistence also included zero and were sensitive to block length. In the post-hoc XGBoost ablation, interruption history and calendar variables achieved an MAE of 54.26, whereas the weather group provided no incremental gain; the variant was not promoted because it was selected after the test results had been observed. In the direct evaluation of 1-, 3-, 7-, and 14-day horizons, using 352 common target dates, XGBoost had the lowest observed MAE at $h=1$ (62.86), whereas Bi-GRU had the lowest at $h=3$ (70.72), $h=7$ (68.17), and $h=14$ (67.50); all three models remained below persistence at all four horizons. The results show that ranking depends on the metric and forecast lead time and that weather association does not imply incremental predictive gain or causality. The study contributes an auditable dataset and a reproducible protocol for temporal validation, multi-horizon comparison, uncertainty, ablation, and residual diagnostics.
